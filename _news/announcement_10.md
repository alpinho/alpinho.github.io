Check out our freshly posted preprint on bioRxiv, <a href="https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.21.768330"><i>Shared and context-dependent cortico--striatal--cerebellar organization of temporal prediction</i></a>&thinsp;! Does the brain keep a steady beat with different circuits than it uses to time a single interval? We put this long-standing idea to the test by scanning the same 31 people across a range of timing tasks. We found that one shared architecture spanning the cortex, striatum and cerebellum does the job across the board. The striatum, however, has its own preferences: it is most engaged when timing turns into action, and it favours the auditory beat itself rather than just anything predictable.
